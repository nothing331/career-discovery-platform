Startup Design / Phase 4

A clearer decision.
A smaller first test.

Phase 4 working strategy, grounded in the completed research and your free-launch brief. This is a proposal for review before brand work.

Help students check a realistic Germany study path.

Working positioning [Opinion]: for Indian students exploring or checking a German bachelor’s or master’s plan, explain which options deserve further attention, what assumptions or requirements remain unresolved, and which official evidence to inspect next.

Keep from your visionFocus the first pilotStill unproven
Both study levels; optional profile + conversation; free launch; evidence students can cross-check.A concrete requirement, cost or pathway decision. Use manually reviewed examples and compare with free alternatives.Whether the result helps more, whether students return, what delivery costs, and whether anyone will pay later.

Three choices for the first version

  • Promise clarity, rather than a best-country verdict. Germany is the supported destination. The wider country roadmap needs separate evidence.
  • Let uncertainty stay visible. Distinguish a relevant option, a missing-information case and a verified requirement conflict. Admission and visa decisions remain separate.
  • Launch as a learning service. Keep the student price at zero, measure human work, and automate only the repeatable parts that improve the result.

What is ready to do next

Review the target student, positioning, free model and pilot sequence in these chapters. Once that direction is agreed, Brand and the detailed Product phase can build on it. No student outreach, prototype admissions evaluation or paid forecast has been performed.

Startup Design / Phase 4

A month of evidence.
Then a build decision.

All counts and timings below are proposed planning targets [Assumption], not completed activities, promises or statistical validation.

Week 1 / Define and recruit

  • Set the cash/time cap and identify who can review material requirements.
  • Inventory actual founder access and one or two plausible gatekeepers; seek permission before recruiting.
  • Prepare neutral prompts and a source-check log for the four interview cells.
  • Choose a deliberate age/consent approach before identifiable student testing.

Week 2 / Learn the decision

  • Work toward 12 voluntary interviews, aspirationally three per degree × decision-stage cell. Targets depend on access.
  • Ask about a recent real task, current sources and the unresolved decision before demonstrating a result.
  • Capture anonymized exact language separately from interpretation; allow participants to skip financial or family detail.
  • Choose the first subject and intake examples from actual cases and source auditability.

Week 3 / Compare a useful result

  • Run up to eight free, manually verified cases across both study levels where recruitment permits.
  • Compare the same task with the student’s current workflow and the official free baseline.
  • Record material errors, source freshness, missing information, evidence opened, next action and human minutes.
  • Correct unsupported conclusions before sending another result; keep lower-detail output appropriately general.

Week 4 / Decide the next investment

  • Invite voluntary follow-up about the named next action; record no response as missing.
  • Review useful outcomes and errors alongside real costs and effort.
  • Choose a narrower repeatable slice or revise the offer if no added value appears.
  • Decide whether to draft/build the scoped MVP, continue manual learning or park the idea; keep the country roadmap separate.

A neutral interview script

  • “What study decision were you working on most recently?”
  • “Walk me through what you tried and the sources you used.”
  • “Which part is still uncertain, and what would change your decision?”
  • “Which personal details would you be comfortable adding, and why?”
  • After a prototype result: “What would you do next? Which claim would you check?”
  • Then compare with the baseline: “What, if anything, became clearer here?”
